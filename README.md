## Abstract
Text recognition of emotions by means of various algorithms has gained significant popularity in areas such as marketing and advertising, psychology, customer analytics, and security systems, helping firms acquire a better understanding of users' behavior and thus take more efficient actions. At the same time, algorithms for emotion detection on textual data perform excellently well with clean and/or synthetically created datasets while having poor results on real data obtained from social networks and other sources. The major issues include subjective and time-consuming annotation of datasets, noise in user-generated texts, frequent use of slang and misspellings, imbalance between the number of emotional classes, limited expressivity of emotions in short texts, and situational and cultural specificity of emotions making the same words mean different things in different situations.
Given the challenges presented above, the primary objective of this research will be to develop and validate an end-to-end Emotion AI pipeline capable of categorizing authentic and noisy social media posts written in informal language based on seven emotions as outlined by the extended Ekman theory.
